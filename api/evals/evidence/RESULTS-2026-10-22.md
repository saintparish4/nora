# Results: the plan of 2026-10-22

Every condition in `PLAN-2026-10-22.md` was run as written; nothing was added
or changed after the runs started. Model: gpt-4o-mini, served
`gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18`, 3 reps, 93 calls, about 64k prompt and 46k
completion tokens (about $0.04). No errors, no truncation, every excerpt
verbatim.

Checks before reading anything:

- R (`rules`) reproduces v3 exactly on all 31 cases.
- Deriving rules-plus-model from `model_baseline`'s own excerpts reproduces its
  scores exactly (93 of 93 rows). The derivation reproduces the service's
  duplicate rule.
- Deriving rules-plus-model with the rule round as the "model" reproduces R.

## Decision, by the rule fixed in advance

- No condition qualifies to replace the rules. None has its `clean`
  interval above −5 points, and no `found` gain is resolved.
- **M, RM, G and F are ruled out on these cases**: each is clearly worse on
  `clean`, and RM, G and F also on `precision`.
- **B is not distinguishable from the rules** on any metric. "Not
  distinguishable" here means intervals 45 points wide on `clean`, not
  equivalence.

## What the failure types show (per-type counts, `eval:evidence:taxonomy`)

Read from both slices; cells are tiny, so these are descriptions, not tests.

- **ConText fixes what it models and nothing else.** With the filter, the
  model's errors on negation (test: 5/15 to 12/15 clean), relatives
  (5/9 to 9/9) and silent charts went away. Errors on hypothetical,
  timing, threshold and wrong-document requirements did not move: 0 of 3 each
  on the test slice, before and after. ConText has no notion of "advised",
  "declined", a six-month window, a BMI cut-off, or which document may carry a
  fact.
- **The plan predicted two costs for the filter. Both held.**
  - It does not catch hypotheticals.
  - It drops correct excerpts that lack the hint vocabulary. Its only lost
    finds (test slice, every rep) are two "no other GLP-1" statements that
    never say "GLP-1":
    - "She is not on semaglutide, liraglutide, or any similar injectable"
      (`unusual_wording`)
    - "Saxenda will be stopped before the first Wegovy dose; the two will not
      be taken together" (`switching_from_glp1`)
    
    The per-type table files these under negation and timing, not `wording`,
    because the tags record the deciding phenomenon and a requirement can
    carry several. The tags missed that these two are also unusual wordings.
- **The medspaCy baseline fails exactly where it was predicted to.**
  - Hypotheticals: train 2/4 clean. It proposed two declined drugs as trials,
    and an advised diet as a program.
  - Timing: test 0/1. It has no duration check, so it proposed a two-month
    program.
  
  Everywhere else it matched the hand-written rules, with no tuning of its
  own. It shares their vocabulary, so this says ConText's modifiers do about
  as well as the rules' hand-written exclusions on these cases. It does not
  say medspaCy alone would.
- **Gating cannot help `clean`.** This was not foreseen when the plan was
  written. The rules propose nothing on any unsupported requirement (that is
  their clean of 100), so "model only where the rules found nothing" lets
  every model excerpt through on exactly those requirements. G equals RM on
  `clean` by construction; it can only change things on supported
  requirements.
- **The model's one real contribution:** three supported requirements the
  rules miss on the test slice, found in all three conditions that include it.
  Even uncorrected, the gain is not resolved (+5.7 [0.0, 15.7]).

## What these numbers cannot support

- **Anything about real charts.** The charts are synthetic, short, and
  written by the person who wrote the rules.
- **Anything about these payers.** 45% of the labelled requirements test
  criteria that the case's payer does not publish (`policies/DIFF.md`).
- **A difference smaller than about 40 points on `clean`.** There are 9 test
  cases (`PROTOCOL.md` part 5: about 100 or more are needed for 10 points).
- **An untouched test slice for the model conditions.** The test slice was
  read before this plan (disclosed there), so model results on it are weaker
  evidence than a sealed set would give.
- **The rules being better than medspaCy, or the reverse.**
- **Anything about a stronger model or a different prompt.** Only gpt-4o-mini
  with the current prompt was run.
- **The failure-type tags.** One author assigned them; no second reader has
  checked them.
